# Content system

The product brief says what the company is. The pillars say what this company is willing to say in public, and which proof it can name. Channel voice says how that sounds on each network. Every public draft reads both files. A vague pillar makes every draft generic.

Pillars: `/var/lib/plow/workspace/content-pillars.md`
Channel voice: `/var/lib/plow/workspace/channel-voice.md`

Only the owner writes these files, and only in the owner's conversation.

If the pillars file is missing when someone asks for a post, a reply, a launch, or outreach, ask once for three to five pillars. Each pillar needs a theme, the audience pain, proof that already exists, two angles, and what this pillar will not say. Also ask how the company should sound, how it must never sound, words it never uses, the one primary channel, and the cadence. Write both files from those answers. Unused channels get one starter line, not a fake voice. Do not start a worker in that turn.

Keep the files short. When the owner corrects a pillar, a banned phrase, or the primary channel, edit the file. Refresh the pillars when the product or the audience changes, or when nothing they post is getting a reply.

The pillars are not permission to publish. Yolo does not skip them. It skips the ask, and only after the editor passes.

## How a draft is shaped

X is one hook, then one thought. A thread is several posts, one idea each, and the owner sets the maximum. Links go in a reply unless the channel voice says otherwise.

LinkedIn opens with a first line that stands alone, about 210 characters. Do not open with an announcement cliché. Short paragraphs. A link goes in the first comment unless the channel voice says otherwise.

Long-form starts with one sentence the reader should believe, who it is for, and an outline of three to seven sections. Then the draft. Then cut it. One idea per paragraph. Stories come from material the owner actually supplied.

Humor stays off unless the owner set a dial in the channel voice. Zero means never. A joke has to carry the point. No jokes about a crisis, a layoff, or a person smaller than the company.

If the owner pasted notes from a call, a message, or a week, that paste is the only source for "what happened." Do not invent the meeting.
