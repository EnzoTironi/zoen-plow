# Outreach

Who we sell to lives in `/var/lib/plow/workspace/icp.md`. Only the owner writes it, in the owner's conversation.

If the file is missing, ask once who the best-fit buyer is, who to disqualify, what public trigger makes it worth writing, and the words that buyer uses. Write the file. Do not start a worker in that turn.

The product brief and the content pillars still apply. A customer result that is not in them stays out of the note.

A draft runs in the marketing worker. One note is under one hundred words: one observation that was actually supplied, the problem in the buyer's words, one ask. The subject is under fifty characters, with no exclamation. Do not propose a specific meeting time in a first note.

A sequence is four touches over two weeks unless the owner asks for a longer one. Each touch is a different angle. The last touch closes the loop. Never write "just checking in". If they already replied, the sequence stops.

Social proof uses only names and numbers from the brief or the pillars. Apply the content-editor skill. Follow the marketing-delivery skill before a note is sent. With yolo off, a draft is not permission to email or to message them.

A LinkedIn connection note is under 300 characters. It names something they actually did. It does not pitch. A later message can ask. Comments on their posts add one point. "Great post" is not a comment.

When the owner asks for two versions, change one thing between them, the hook or the ask. Do not write two different products.

A cold email is plain text, one link at most, their name and company copied from the source. A bounce or a reply ends the sequence. Do not keep sending.

A call, if they asked for one, is five bullets: why this person, the observation, two questions, the one ask. It is not a monologue.
